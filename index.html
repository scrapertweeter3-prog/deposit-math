Three small deposits and a lot of waiting

A note from Extra Hustles

Bank bonus write-ups love the payout: open an account, park some money, collect $300. What almost none of them price is the verification step. Most bonus terms that require direct deposits also require the bank to recognize them, and recognition is where the unpaid hours go.

The mechanism is fussy. Some banks match deposit descriptions against a keyword list, so a transfer labeled "transfer from savings" reads as nothing at all. Others run a two-step dance: small trial credits first, a few cents each, and the real direct deposit only after those post. Payroll cycles run on a schedule that ignores your deadline, so a miss means waiting for the next cycle, and that can be a month of calendar time spent watching a dashboard.

Put hours on it. Suppose the term is two direct deposits inside 90 days. Suppose your paymaster's first attempt bounces off a keyword matcher and the retry has to wait for the next payroll cycle. The bonus still clears, but the labor now includes: reading the terms to learn the keywords, running the trial credits, correcting the deposit description, and waiting out a cycle. Call the paperwork 2 hours and the wait 4 to 5 weeks of low-grade attention. Priced by active hours the bonus pays about $150 an hour, which is why the churn works at all. Priced across the weeks you spend minding it, it lands closer to $65 a week. Only the first number ever makes it into the write-ups.

The fix is cheap and mostly unread: the account agreement already contains the keyword list and the trial-credit rule, and it is free to read. Verify what your paymaster can send and what the bank's matcher accepts before the clock starts. Ten minutes with the agreement beats a month of dashboard watching, and a bonus that clears on the first cycle pays the same as one that cleared on the third.
